## Content
> "A funded survey gives you a perfect snapshot of one beach on one day. Citizen science gives you a blurry photo of every beach, every day. For detecting *change* — which is the entire game in ecology — the blurry-everywhere dataset wins. We spent a decade learning that coverage beats precision when you're trying to catch a system moving."

## Core Insight
For detecting change over time, broad noisy coverage beats narrow precise snapshots — frequency and geographic spread matter more than per-observation accuracy.

## Relevance to My Thinking
This is the load-bearing argument under TidePool. The skeptic's objection is "phone photos from amateurs are noisy" — Okonkwo's point is that noise is fine if coverage is high and you're measuring deltas, not absolutes.
